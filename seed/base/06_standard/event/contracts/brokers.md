# Brokers

- **Two families, one decision:** the **replayable log** (Kafka, Redpanda — partitioned, ordered-per-key, consumers track offsets, history retained) and the **routing queue** (RabbitMQ — broker routes to queues, messages die on ack, rich routing/TTL/priority). The family is chosen by the questions "will anyone ever replay?" and "who owns delivery state?" — not by throughput folklore.
- **The log family when:** events feed multiple independent consumers at their own pace · projections/read models must be rebuildable from history · per-entity ordering matters (partition by key) · the event stream IS a product (analytics, audit). Retention is a feature: the log remembers, consumers come and go.
- **Redpanda is Kafka's API without Kafka's ops:** protocol-compatible (clients/tooling identical), single binary, no JVM/ZooKeeper — the house pick when the log family wins and the ops budget is one team; choosing Kafka itself is choosing its ecosystem weight deliberately (managed service or existing platform).
- **RabbitMQ when routing is the feature:** work distribution with acks/redelivery, per-message TTL, priorities, dead-letter exchanges, topic/fanout routing decided broker-side — task-queue shapes, RPC-ish flows, and fan-out where consumers must NOT re-read history. It tracks delivery per message; consumers stay dumb about position.
- **Redis Streams is the honest starter:** consumer groups, pending lists, claim/auto-claim — the log family's semantics at Redis-ops cost; the right FIRST broker for a monolith already running Redis, with the port making a later Redpanda swap a driver file, not a rewrite.
- **The abstraction holds regardless:** `Event::publish(name, payload, key)` through the port — the KEY is sacred in every family (partition routing in the log, ordering hint in queues); business code never imports a broker client, and the outbox/idempotency laws apply identically because delivery is at-least-once EVERYWHERE.
- **What the family changes downstream, written into the consumer:** log consumers own their offset and MUST tolerate re-reading history (restarts replay) · queue consumers own ack/nack discipline and MUST bound redelivery (poison → DLX) · both dedup by event id; neither may assume global order — only the log gives per-key order, and only within a partition.
- **Ordering truth table:** log family = ordered per partition key, never across partitions · RabbitMQ = ordered per queue until concurrency or redelivery breaks it · Redis Streams = ordered per stream. Design consumers for per-entity order at most; a workflow needing more is a saga, not a broker setting.
- **Broker migration is a designed path, not a crisis:** publish through the port from day one, keep envelopes broker-neutral, and a family switch is drivers + consumer-semantics review (offsets vs acks) — the systems that cannot leave their broker are the ones that let its vocabulary leak inward.
- Capacity honesty per family: the log scales by partitions (chosen at topic design — repartitioning is surgery: over-provision partitions early) · queues scale by consumers-per-queue · Redis Streams scales to one node's memory — write the growth assumption next to the topic/queue declaration.
